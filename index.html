Low-energy Muon-Nucleon scattering Experiment

Precision muon scattering at the high-intensity frontier

Based on the high-intensity muon beam at HIAF (0.5–7.5 GeV/c), LUNE will perform precision scattering measurements with both μ+ and μ− beams, bridging the gap between low-energy electron facilities and future high-energy lepton–ion colliders. The experiment will determine the proton (deuteron) charge radius, measure nucleon electromagnetic form factors, and study the dynamics of quantum electrodynamics and hadronic interactions.

Measurement Principle

The muon is a point-like lepton approximately 207 times heavier than the electron. The angular distribution of elastic muon–proton scattering is governed by the proton electromagnetic form factors, which encode the spatial distributions of the proton's (deuteron's) charge.

Elastic scattering & form factors

At low momentum transfer (low Q2), the slope of the elastic cross section versus Q2 determines the proton charge radius. The cross section is governed by the electric and magnetic form factors GE(Q2) and GM(Q2); precision small-angle measurements extrapolated to Q2 → 0 yield the radius.

Why muons?

With their large mass and penetrating power, muons probe the internal structure of nuclei. Comparing μ+ and μ− scattering provides an independent handle on two-photon exchange (TPE) effects — an important quantum electrodynamics correction in electron scattering.

Staged program

Phase I: low-Q2 elastic μ–p scattering with a dipole spectrometer, targeting a 1.0% determination of the proton charge radius. Phase II: an upgrade to a 2 T solenoidal spectrometer enabling DIS, SIDIS, and DVCS studies of the 3D nucleon structure and searches for physics beyond the Standard Model.

Physics Objectives

The LUNE program addresses several long-standing questions in nuclear and particle physics, from the proton charge radius to the structure of the nucleon.

The proton charge radius puzzle

Values of the proton charge radius extracted from electron scattering, electron spectroscopy, and muonic hydrogen differ by about 4%. LUNE will provide an independent determination using a muon probe, a critical input for resolving this discrepancy.

Two-photon exchange & tests of QED

Systematic comparisons of μ+ and μ− beams enable controlled studies of two-photon exchange effects and stringent tests of quantum electrodynamics and the radiative correction framework.

Three-dimensional nucleon structure

The high-statistics Phase-II DIS, SIDIS, and DVCS samples will provide access to transverse momentum dependent distributions (TMDs), generalized parton distributions (GPDs), gravitational form factors, and the orbital dynamics of quarks in the nucleon.

Nuclear structure & medium effects

Precision charge radii of light nuclei, Coulomb distortion corrections, nuclear medium modifications (the EMC effect), and — in the longer term — short-range correlations (SRC).

Beyond the Standard Model

The high-intensity muon beam also allows searches for new physics, including charged-lepton-flavor violation, hadronic contributions relevant to the muon g−2, and precision searches for light new particles such as dark photons.

Detector Systems

The HIAF-HIRIBL muon beamline (192 m, ±2% momentum acceptance) feeds a staged detector system. Phase I is optimized for precision low-Q2 elastic scattering; Phase II extends the acceptance and particle identification with barrel detectors inside a superconducting solenoid. See the White Paper (Sec. 3) for full details.

HIAF μ beam — 0.5–7.5 GeV/c · ~106 μ/s · 2–10 cm spot · 10 mrad divergence · naturally polarized (under study)

Phase I — Dipole Spectrometer

Proton charge radius · low-Q2 elastic scattering
  • Target: 5.0 mm CH2, with a 2.24 mm graphite target of equivalent carbon areal density for background subtraction.
  • Tracking: MAPS silicon pixel detectors (MIC6-v4, CCNU), < 0.5% X0 per layer, 30 μm pixels; momentum resolution < 2.0% (0.25–2 GeV/c), angular resolution < 0.8 mrad, efficiency > 97%.
  • Magnet: ~0.5 T room-temperature dipole, field uniformity < ±5 × 10−3.
  • PID: μ/π separation > 90% (> 95% desirable); ECal resolution < 8%/√E ⊕ C.

Phase II — Solenoidal Spectrometer

DIS · SIDIS · DVCS · nuclear structure
  • Target: 5 cm / 50 cm liquid hydrogen (LH2) target for maximum luminosity.
  • Solenoid: 2 T superconducting magnet, 150 cm length, 80 cm diameter bore, 20 cm radial thickness.
  • Drift chamber: 12 cylindrical layers (r = 15–37.4 cm), Ar–CO2 (70:30), transverse resolution ~100–150 μm, forming a hybrid tracking system with the silicon pixels.
  • Calorimetry & μ-ID: extended Shashlyk electromagnetic calorimetry and a forward RPC-based muon identification system.
